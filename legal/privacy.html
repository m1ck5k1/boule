Privacy policy

Boule is a second tally that runs beside the real one, so the gap becomes visible. To do that it records how people would vote on real bills. That is a political opinion, so this policy is deliberately short and specific.

What Boule is

Boule is a public, non-binding record of how the public would vote on legislation moving through the United States Congress. It is not a government service, it is not an official tally, and it has no authority over anything. It exists to measure the distance between what Congress decides and what the public would have decided — we call that distance the gap.

What we collect

There is no sign-in, and no account

Boule does not ask who you are. There is no account, no email address, no password, and no Google or Microsoft sign-in. We are not in the business of identity, and we do not collect it.

Instead, the installed app is issued a key when you install it, and it keeps it. That key does two things and nothing else: it lets the server recognise that this device has already had its say on a particular bill, and it lets the record label a reading as coming from an installed app rather than a browser. It is not an identity. It holds nothing about you, and it is never published.

When you record a reading

  • which bill, and which version of that bill you read
  • your choice, and any later change of choice — a change adds a record; it never edits one
  • the time the reading reached our server, used to stamp the stage, and coarsened to the day before anything is published
  • a provenance label: device (the installed-app key above) or anonymous (a browser, standing on nothing durable)
  • a hash of your network address, held by the server only to spot abuse — never published, in any form, at any granularity

Technical data

Our server necessarily sees the IP address of any request, as every web server does. See "Not yet decided" below for how long we keep it.

How we use Google user data

For two things only: to show you your own voting record, and to label each vote with its provenance tier so a reader can see how strongly identity was established. That is the entire purpose.

Boule's use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What we never do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No selling, renting, or sharing personal data with anyone.
  • No tracking you across other websites.
  • No transferring Google user data to third parties except as needed to provide Boule, to comply with the law, or as part of a merger — and never for advertising.
  • No automated decision-making about you. Boule does not decide anything; that is the point of it.

What is published

The ledger is published, because a tally nobody can check is just a claim. Published readings carry the bill, the version, the choice, the day (never a precise time), and a random pseudonym re-issued for every bill — so no reading can be linked to a device, or to that same device's reading on any other bill.

Never published: your network address or any hash of it, the device key, any name or email (we hold none), precise times, or anything else that could single out a person. The standard is written field by field in DATA.md, behind a single absolute rule — nothing we publish may be used to identify or target anyone, on any level. The data is here to help, not to be abused.

Retention, and what you can ask for

There is no account to delete, because there is no account. What you can do is reset your device key. Boule's record is append-only and is designed never to be rewritten — that is what makes the tally worth trusting — so the honest position, said plainly rather than buried, is:

  • A reading already published stays published. It carries nothing that identifies you (see above), which is exactly why it can stay: an anonymous count is not a fact about a person.
  • Resetting the key erases nothing — it stops the attribution going forward. This device's future readings stand apart from its past ones.
  • Changing your mind is a new record, not an edit. If you withdraw a reading, Boule adds a record saying so. The earlier record stays, and the current tally ignores it. Nothing is rewritten.

To exercise any of this, or to ask what we hold about you, contact privacy@incidium.net.

Not yet decided

Boule is in active construction. These points are deliberately left open rather than written as promises we might not keep, and this policy is not final until they are settled:

  • Whether the raw network address is discarded immediately, or held briefly to spot abuse. What is already settled: it is kept only as a hash, and it is never published.
  • Whether analytics are collected, and if so they will be aggregate and anonymous — never per-person.

Settled since the first draft of this policy: no email is stored, because none is collected; there is no account and no sign-in; a reading already published stays published, because it carries nothing that could identify anyone; and the published-dataset standard is now written down rather than deferred.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects you, we will say so on the front page rather than editing quietly.

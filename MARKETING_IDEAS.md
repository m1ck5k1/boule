# MARKETING_IDEAS.md

> Blue-sky ideas, and the plan underneath them.
>
> This is not a growth document. Boule has exactly one asset — that its number can be
> trusted — so every decision here is a **credibility** decision wearing a marketing hat.
> Read §0 before using anything below it.

---

## 0. The doctrine

Three rules that override everything else in this file.

**1. Never promote a number we would not defend.**
If the public sample is 40 people, the gap is noise. No gap is shared below a stated
minimum sample, and **the sample size travels with every share, always**, in the same
breath as the percentage. A percentage without its sample is the exact species of claim
this project was built to audit.

**2. No targeting, ever.**
Nothing published may identify or target a person — voters *or* officials. We publish
arithmetic about bills. The moment promotion is aimed at an individual we become the
thing we audit. (This is the public-facing edge of the absolute rule in `DATA.md`.)

**3. Nothing is promoted that the product would not show.**
No poster slogan that the site contradicts. No tagline the ledger cannot back.

Corollary: **we do not buy ads.** Not for purity — because a paid placement is a claim we
paid to make, and the entire product is the claim that our numbers were not paid for.
Paid is parked (§9), not forbidden.

---

## 1. The cold-start unlock

The obvious problem: no voters → no gap → nothing to promote → no voters.

**The fix is to promote the absence, not the number.** Before a single credible gap
exists, the promotable fact is:

> *Congress voted 219–207. Nobody asked the country. No one is keeping that number.*

That sentence is true on day one, needs zero users, and is the actual thesis. Promotion
starts there, not at a percentage.

---

## 2. The recurring hook

A gap is interesting once. A **scoreboard** is interesting weekly. The product must emit
something on a schedule, or there is nothing to say.

**The Friday dispatch:** one bill, two numbers, three sentences, no commentary. Sent
forever — whether or not the gap flatters anyone, and including the weeks it is boring.
Consistency is the brand; the restraint is the voice.

## 3. Blue-sky ideas

Rapid, unranked, 2–3 lines each. Some are absurd. Absurd is fine at this stage — the
cost of the idea is zero and the cost of a missed one is the whole project.

**The Gap Card, auto-generated.** Every time an official vote lands, Boule mints one
image: two bars, the delta, the sample size, one line on what the bill did. Not a post we
write — a scoreboard that writes itself.

**District receipts.** Enter a postcode, get your representative's gap record as a plain
page of arithmetic. Not an attack ad; a receipt. Mail-merge it to paper and it fits
through a door.

**Publish the prediction first.** Post the public tally *before* the official vote closes,
timestamped. When Congress votes, the gap was already on the record — nobody can accuse us
of retrofitting a number to a result.

**Ship both halves.** If the ten biggest gaps all favour one side, Boule is dead on
arrival. Publish the top ten on each side, every week, whether or not it flatters. The
fairness *is* the campaign.

**The gallery sign.** One sheet of paper, one QR, held up in a public gallery or at a town
hall — scanning it opens the live tally of the bill under debate *at that minute*.
Timeliness no poster can copy.

**Bathroom-stall distribution.** Paper QR cards in pubs, campuses, laundrettes. Cheap,
offline, and the obscurity is part of the joke.

**Give journalists the citation, not the story.** A page that produces one quotable gap
stat with a source line ready to paste. They distribute; we remove their excuse not to.

**Publish the misses.** A permanent page of every bill where the gap was zero, and every
time the public was plainly wrong. Nobody believes a scoreboard that only shows wins.

**A tracker anyone can embed.** One line of HTML gives a blog or local paper a live gap
for the bill they are covering. Hundreds of small sites carry our number for free.

**The absence is the headline.** *"The public's verdict on every bill exists nowhere. We
started keeping it."* The newsworthy fact is not the platform — it is that the number was
missing.

**Send Congress its own report card.** Pick one representative, send their record, publish
the exchange including the silence. Either response is a story.

**Let the data nerds audit us.** Hand the corpus to the GovTrack/GovInfo crowd and ask
them to break it. Being corrected publicly is cheaper than being ignored.

**A billboard with no name on it.** Two numbers, one delta, one QR. No logo, no slogan.
The restraint reads as confidence.

**Never say "come vote."** Always *"Congress voted 219–207. The country said 72%."* The
invitation is curiosity; participation is what happens next.

**An anti-astroturf pledge.** State exactly what Boule cannot prove — one person, one
vote, and why. The only civic site publishing its own weaknesses.

**Eponymous metrics get cited.** "The Whitford Gap." Anonymous numbers get forgotten;
named ones get argued about.

## 4. Who this is actually for

Not "everyone". Three audiences, with different asks.

**A. Journalists and civic-data people.** They want a number they can cite with a source,
on a deadline. Ask: publish the citation page. **They are our distribution.**

**B. The already-engaged citizen** — people who read bill text for fun. They want the
record and a shareable artefact. Ask: vote, then share one card.

**C. Researchers and archivists.** They want the whole ledger, downloadable, citable.
Ask: break it, then cite it. Slow, but their citation is permanent.

The general public is not an audience for *promotion* — they are the audience for the
*product*, arriving through A and B.

## 5. The plan, in phases with gates

Each phase has a **pass/fail gate** fixed before it starts, matching the house style used
for S0–S8. No phase begins until the previous gate passes.

**Phase 1 — Be findable and correct.**
Register the domain, TLS, `/about` stating the thesis in one line, the published ledger
downloadable, and `/cite` live.
*Gate:* a stranger lands, understands it in 10 seconds, and can find the method.

**Phase 2 — The first hundred real voters.**
Recruit by hand. No growth tactics: personal invitations, and a post in the places people
already argue about legislation.
*Gate:* 100 voters on at least one bill that has a real official vote, and the number
survives scrutiny from someone hostile.

**Phase 3 — The first independent citation.**
Pitch five journalists, one researcher, one civic-data newsletter — as a *source*, not as
a story.
*Gate:* one citation from someone with no relationship to us.

**Phase 4 — The shareable artefact.**
Gap Cards, auto-generated, sample size baked in.
*Gate:* a card shared by someone we have never spoken to.

**Phase 5 — Physical.**
QR cards, the gallery sign, the anonymous billboard.
*Gate:* a scan we did not ask for, at a location we did not choose.

**Phase 6 — Recurring.**
The Friday dispatch, the weekly top-ten-both-sides.
*Gate:* six consecutive weeks shipped without a break. Consistency is the only proof of
seriousness.

## 6. Channels, with the actual first action

Ideas are cheap; the first action is the deliverable.

**The repo itself.** It is public. Every gate result, every failed experiment, every
"Moments" entry is content, and it is the only marketing asset that is also the work.
*First action:* bring `README.md` up to date — it currently undersells the project badly
(§11).

**Journalism (primary channel).**
*First action:* build `/cite` — a page that generates a gap statistic with a source line
and the sample size, ready to paste.

**Civic-data communities.** GovTrack-adjacent forums, open-data mailing lists.
*First action:* post the ledger, explicitly asking to be proved wrong.

**Short-form social.** One card, no thread, no hashtag soup.
*First action:* post a Gap Card with the sample size and nothing else.

**Physical.**
*First action:* print 200 QR cards and put them somewhere boring.

**Search.** People search "how did my senator vote".
*First action:* one honest page per bill, indexable, with the official tally first.

## 7. What we measure

The project's own discipline applies: **rank by usefulness to the gap, not by engagement.**

| Measure | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Bills with a **credible** gap (above minimum sample) | this is the product existing |
| **Independent citations** | the only promotion that compounds |
| **Return rate** of voters across bills | whether it is a habit or a novelty |
| Share → first vote conversion | whether the card actually works |

Deliberately **not** measured: pageviews, followers, time-on-site. *A feature that makes
people stay longer has earned nothing.*

## 8. Ways this goes wrong

**It gets read as a campaign.**
*Mitigation:* publish both halves; never name an opponent; the voice is arithmetic.

**The sample is too small and the number is noise.**
*Mitigation:* the minimum-sample rule, and the sample on every card. This is the failure
most likely to kill the project's credibility, and it is entirely self-inflicted.

**Someone stuffs the tally, and that becomes the story.**
*Mitigation:* publish the anomaly readings alongside — the opinion layer exists precisely
for this. And the ledger is public, so stuffing is visible rather than hidden.

**The joke undercuts the seriousness.**
*Mitigation:* `Boule Schit` is a **social-register handle only.** It never appears in a data
release byline, in a citation, or in the product masthead. The joke is a footnote in the
product and a banner on the poster — never both at once.

**We promote a number we later correct.**
*Mitigation:* publish corrections on the front page rather than quietly. A corrected
number is stronger than an uncorrected one.

## 9. Parked, with the trigger to unpark

Nothing here is forbidden; each waits on a condition.

- **Paid advertising** — unpark when the gap is credible *and* the bottleneck is reach
  rather than trust. Probably never.
- **Merch** — unpark when someone asks for it unprompted.
- **Podcast / video** — unpark when the Friday dispatch has a six-week streak.
- **Influencer placement** — unpark only if they cite us first.
- **A second jurisdiction** — unpark when one jurisdiction is boring.

## 11. Product dependencies — this plan cannot be executed alone

Marketing is downstream of product here, and two of these are **blocking**, not nice to have.

**A. The minimum-sample rule is a product change, not a marketing one.**
§0.1 is unenforceable while the renderer will happily draw a gap from eleven votes. The
threshold must live in the engine next to the retention windows in `engine/retention.py`
— one place, not a number scattered through prose — and no Gap Card may be generated below
it. Until that exists, §0.1 is a promise this document cannot keep.

**B. The README is the most-read and least-current file in the repo.**
Phase 1 requires a stranger to understand the project in ten seconds. Right now the README
says the status is *"pivot in progress"* and that **"the voting layer itself"** is *"not
built yet"* — while S3 is merged and S4 is drawing real gaps against real Senate roll
calls. It is not merely stale; it actively understates the work.

**C. `/cite` does not exist yet**, and Phase 3 depends entirely on it.

The single sentence this whole document serves:

> **The gap only means something if nobody can accuse us of manufacturing it.**
